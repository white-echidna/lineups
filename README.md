# Lineups

> Graven with an iron pen and lead in the rock for ever.

There is collection of useful linups for _Killjoy_'s mollys.

## About Trajectories

Like any standard projectile, a molly moves through the air along a parabolic arc.
This means its flight path is governed entirely by three fundamental variables:

- the agent's spatial coordinates,
- their velocity at the moment of release,
- and the crosshair's aim vector.

Because of this mathematical nature, pinpoint positioning forms the bedrock of every successful
lineup; even a minor displacement on the ground can ripple into a wide miss at the landing site.

In high-pressure rounds, reliability outweighs theoretical perfection.
While the physics engine allows for an endless variety of trajectories,
a practical collection should focus on setups that are robust against slight execution errors.

The optimal choice is always dictated by the round's context: in tense post-plant situations,
you want a lineup that lands reliably even if your timing or placement is slightly off.

## About throw Styles

Consistency in execution comes from a strict sequence of actions:

1. first settle into your exact stance,
2. align your crosshair with the environmental reference,
3. initiate the jump,
4. and finally release the projectile at the predetermined frame.

While a molly can technically be released at any point during a jump,
three fundamental styles cover almost every practical need:

- Standing Throw (`No-jump`): The most predictable approach, completely immune to timing errors.

- `Jump-throw`: Released immediately upon jumping,
  transferring peak upward momentum to achieve maximum launch distance.

- `Peak-jump`: Released as the agent nears the apex of their jump.

Executing a true apex throw presents a natural challenge,
as the window of zero vertical velocity is fleeting.
For this reason, standard peak-jump lineups assume a release just before reaching the crest,
while the agent still carries a faint upward momentum.

A rarer yet distinct alternative is the `Descending Peak-jump`,
where the projectile is unleashed just after the apex on the way down.
This subtle delay radically alters the landing angle and arc,
making it a niche yet powerful tool for clearing tight sightlines and obstacles.
